Research Questions for the Physics-Guided LLE Proposal

Research questions to add to "Physics-Guided Loss Functions for Low-Light Image Restoration"

================================================================================

Suggested Placement

Insert a "Research Questions" section between Section 2 (Problem Statement) and
Section 3 (Research Objectives). Research questions frame what the study seeks to
answer; objectives then state what concrete steps will be taken to answer them.
This ordering gives the reader a clear logical arc: gap -> questions -> objectives
-> methodology.

================================================================================

Primary Research Question

RQ1: To what extent can physics-guided loss functions -- encoding Retinex
decomposition, blur formation physics, and Fourier-domain phase structure --
improve the quality, generalization, and physical plausibility of deep
learning-based low-light image restoration, without requiring architectural
modifications?

Rationale: This overarching question captures the central hypothesis: that
explicit physics-based training constraints can meaningfully improve a
state-of-the-art model (DarkIR) trained with generic losses. The "without
architectural modifications" qualifier is important -- it scopes the contribution
to loss design rather than network design, which is the proposal's distinct angle.

================================================================================

Sub-Research Questions

RQ2 -- Retinex Decomposition Regularization

Does enforcing illumination smoothness and reconstruction consistency
(I = R * L) through differentiable loss terms yield more physically plausible
illumination-reflectance decompositions and measurably better restoration quality
than training with generic pixel-wise and perceptual losses alone?

Testable via: Ablation experiment 1 (Baseline vs. +Retinex). Measured by
PSNR/SSIM on LOLBlur test, plus qualitative inspection of extracted illumination
maps for smoothness and absence of high-frequency artifacts.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

RQ3 -- Blur-Aware Gradient Consistency

Does enforcing gradient consistency under re-blurring -- requiring that the
deblurred output, when convolved with an estimated PSF, matches the gradient
statistics of the input -- reduce unnatural sharpening artifacts and improve
deblurring fidelity compared to edge-only supervision?

Testable via: Ablation experiment 2 (Baseline vs. +Blur). Measured by PSNR/LPIPS
on LOLBlur, edge preservation IoU, and visual comparison of sharpening artifacts
on challenging motion-blur cases.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

RQ4 -- Phase-Enhanced Frequency Supervision

Does augmenting amplitude-based frequency losses with explicit phase gradient
consistency preserve edge and structural detail during illumination enhancement,
and does this yield measurable improvement over amplitude-only supervision?

Testable via: Ablation experiment 3 (Baseline vs. +Freq). Measured by LPIPS
(perceptual detail), SSIM (structural similarity), and frequency-domain analysis
comparing phase spectra of predictions vs. ground truth.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

RQ5 -- Complementarity of Physics Priors

Are the three proposed physics-guided losses complementary -- i.e., does their
combination yield gains beyond what any individual or pairwise combination
achieves -- and do they address distinct aspects of low-light image degradation?

Testable via: Full ablation matrix (experiments 0-7). If the full method
(experiment 7) consistently outperforms all subsets, complementarity is supported.
Interaction effects can be quantified by comparing individual gains vs. combined
gains.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

RQ6 -- Cross-Domain Generalization

Do physics-guided losses improve zero-shot generalization to unseen datasets and
degradation types (LOLv2-Real, LOLv2-Synthetic, LSRW-Huawei, LSRW-Nikon),
suggesting that physics-based regularization encodes transferable priors rather
than dataset-specific patterns?

Testable via: Cross-dataset evaluation (Section 4.4.2). Measured by the
performance gap between in-domain (LOLBlur) and out-of-domain benchmarks for the
baseline vs. full method. A narrower gap indicates better generalization.

================================================================================

Compact Formulation (for space-constrained versions)

If the proposal has page limits, the questions can be condensed:

  RQ1: Can physics-guided losses improve low-light restoration quality and
       generalization without architectural changes?
  RQ2: Does Retinex-based regularization improve illumination modeling over
       generic losses?
  RQ3: Does blur-aware gradient matching reduce deblurring artifacts beyond
       edge-only supervision?
  RQ4: Does phase-enhanced frequency loss preserve structural detail during
       illumination enhancement?
  RQ5: Are the three physics-guided losses complementary?
  RQ6: Do physics-based losses improve cross-dataset generalization?

Sample Prose for Insertion into Proposal

The following text can be inserted as Section 2.5 (before the current Section 3):

  2.5 Research Questions

  Given the limitations outlined above, this study seeks to answer the following
  questions:

  RQ1: To what extent can physics-guided loss functions -- encoding Retinex
  decomposition, blur formation physics, and Fourier-domain phase structure --
  improve the quality, generalization, and physical plausibility of deep
  learning-based low-light image restoration, without requiring architectural
  modifications?

  This primary question is decomposed into four specific sub-questions:

  RQ2: Does enforcing illumination smoothness and reconstruction consistency
  through Retinex-based loss terms yield more physically plausible decompositions
  and better restoration quality than generic losses alone?

  RQ3: Does gradient consistency under re-blurring improve deblurring fidelity
  and reduce unnatural sharpening artifacts compared to edge-only supervision?

  RQ4: Does augmenting amplitude-based frequency losses with phase gradient
  consistency preserve structural detail during illumination enhancement?

  RQ5: Are the proposed physics-guided losses complementary, and do they improve
  zero-shot generalization to unseen datasets and degradation types?

  These questions are directly addressed through the ablation studies and
  cross-dataset experiments described in Section 4.4.

================================================================================

Notes

- RQ2-RQ4 are each directly testable via a single ablation toggle, making them
  experimentally clean.
- RQ5 addresses a higher-level concern (complementarity) that reviewers often ask
  about when multiple loss terms are proposed.
- RQ6 is merged into RQ5 in the prose version to keep the count manageable, but
  can be split out if the committee prefers more granularity.
- If the committee or supervisor requires a hypothesis-driven framing, each RQ
  can be converted to a hypothesis (e.g., "H2: Retinex-based regularization
  improves PSNR by at least 0.3 dB over the baseline on LOLBlur").
